What this prototype does

Photograph a crack. The app measures it, records where the photo was taken, and recognises that spot again the next time somebody stands there. Once the same crack has been photographed twice, it has a growth rate — and a date by which it needs sealing.

WORKING IN THIS BUILD

  • Finds the crack in a photograph and measures its length in millimetres
  • Reads the GPS out of the photo's own metadata — and writes one in when the camera did not
  • Recognises the same crack tomorrow: position, camera heading, and a fingerprint of the scene itself
  • Turns repeat photographs into a growth rate and a sealing date
  • Ranks the seal list by growth × rainfall × traffic

NOT IN THIS BUILD, AND WE SAY SO

  • No trained damage classifier yet. The crack is found by image processing, not by a model
  • Rainfall uses published monthly normals, not a live forecast. Every answer says which it used
  • Traffic uses road-class defaults until a municipality supplies counts
  • Daylight, close range, camera roughly overhead

The seal list

Built only from readings actually taken on this machine. A spot with one photograph has no rate, so it gets no date — it sits at the bottom until it is photographed again.

LEAD TIME — THE FIGURE NOBODY PUBLISHES

Lead time is the gap between the day a crack was first seen and the day it is predicted to cross the sealing threshold. It only exists if somebody photographs the same square metre of road more than once, which is why no Indian road authority reports it today.

HOW THE ORDER IS DECIDED

Growth is measured on this machine, from photographs of that spot. Rain and traffic are multipliers, each with the figure behind it and where it came from printed in the table. A spot with no growth rate yet is not scored at all — it drops to the bottom, because never having been re-photographed is not the same as being safe.
